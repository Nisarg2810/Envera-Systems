Replace a Part With No Drawing: Scan to CAD | Designfusion
Part out of production and no drawings left? Here's how 3D scanning and reverse engineering turn a worn part into a new CAD model and a new part.

        

      

      

        
Additive · Reverse engineering6 min read

        
The part broke and the drawing is gone. Here's how to get it back.

        
By the Designfusion additive team

        

          

            
Every plant has one. A bracket, an impeller, a housing that has done its job quietly for twenty years. Then it cracks, and someone goes looking for the drawing.

            
Sometimes there isn't one. It was drawn by hand, or the CAD file lived on a computer that was retired long ago. Sometimes the drawing exists but the supplier doesn't. The shop that made it closed, was bought out, or will only run a new batch at a minimum order you'll never use.

            
You have three options. Two of them are expensive.

            
1. Measure it by hand and redraw it.A good designer with calipers can rebuild a simple part. But curves, worn surfaces and awkward features are hard to measure, and every guess becomes a fit problem on the first prototype. It's slow, and the person doing it is usually needed somewhere else.

            
2. Replace the whole assembly.If you can't get the part, you can buy a machine or assembly that doesn't need it. That fixes the problem by spending far more than the problem cost.

            
3. Scan it, model it, and make it again.A handheld 3D scanner captures the part's full shape in a few passes, including the curves calipers miss. The scan becomes the starting point for a clean CAD model, and the model becomes a new part.

            
How scan to part works

            
    
              
  1. Capture. The part is scanned at your facility or at ours. A handheld scanner like the Creaform HandySCAN 3D PRO means the part doesn't have to fit on a machine bed.
    2. 
              
  2. Clean up. A raw scan is a mesh: a surface made of millions of tiny triangles. Great for inspection, not much use on the shop floor.
    3. 
              
  3. Rebuild. An engineer uses the mesh as a guide to build a parametric model in software like Siemens NX or Solid Edge. Now you have real dimensions and features you can edit.
    4. 
              
  4. Improve, if you want to. Worn parts usually wore for a reason. This is the moment to thicken a wall, add a rib, or combine two pieces into one. If the new part will be printed, Design for Additive Manufacturing can make it lighter or stronger than the original.
    5. 
              
  5. Make it. Machine it, mould it or print it. For one-offs and short runs, printing in fibre-reinforced or metal materials is often the quickest route.
    6. 
            

            
When scanning is worth it

            
    
              
  • The part is out of production, or the supplier is gone.
    • 
              
  • There's no usable drawing or CAD file.
    • 
              
  • Downtime costs more than the part.
    • 
              
  • The part has failed before and you'd like to improve it.
    • 
              
  • You need a digital record of tooling, fixtures or older equipment.
    • 
            

            
When it isn't

            
If the part is a flat plate with three holes, a tape measure will do. Scanning earns its cost on parts with shape: castings, curved surfaces, worn edges, anything you'd struggle to describe on a drawing.

            
Five questions to ask a scanning provider

            
    
              
  1. Can you come to us, or do we ship the part?
    2. 
              
  2. Do we get a usable CAD model, or only a scan file?
    3. 
              
  3. Which formats: native NX or Solid Edge, or STEP?
    4. 
              
  4. Can you help improve the design, not just copy it?
    5. 
              
  5. Can you make the part too, or only the file?
    6. 
            

            
How Designfusion does it

            
We scan with the Creaform HandySCAN 3D PRO, model in NX and Solid Edge, and can print the finished part on Markforged and Formlabs systems or through our 3D parts brokerage. You choose how far we go: scan data, a full CAD model, or a part redesigned and printed.

The problem

        
The guard housing on Line 2 cracked on a Monday. The filler had been running for almost thirty years, the company that built it no longer existed, and the only drawing was a faded sketch with half the dimensions missing. A machined replacement was quoted at eight weeks, if a shop could work out the geometry at all.

        
What we did

        
Our team scanned the cracked housing on site with the Creaform HandySCAN 3D PRO without taking the filler apart. Back in the office, an engineer rebuilt it as a parametric model in NX. The crack started at a sharp inside corner, so we added a fillet and a rib, and merged the housing and its two brackets into a single part. We printed it in carbon-fibre-reinforced nylon on a Markforged printer.

        
The result

        
The new housing was fitted nine days after the scan. It weighs a third less than the cast original, installs in one piece, and the plant now owns a CAD model it can reprint any time.

        
"We stopped searching for a supplier that didn't exist and had a better part in under two weeks."Maintenance lead, Ontario food packaging plant
