What I found

A close read of borderpass.ai

Before planning anything, I went through the site, its sitemap and its social links. BorderPass already has a lot to work with. These are the eight places where marketing can get more out of what exists.

1

211 blog posts and 72 news posts already live

The sitemap lists a deep library: refusals, reconsideration requests, judicial review timelines, proof of funds, PNP updates, OINP changes, Express Entry, housing for students. Most of it lives only on the blog.

Do this: every post becomes one carousel, one short video, one newsletter item and one LinkedIn post. That alone is years of social content from work already done. The repurposing system is in section 04.
2

Every page shares the same preview image

The PGWP page, the IEC page, the CRS calculator and the homepage all point their og:image to the same generic screenshot file. When someone pastes a PGWP link into WhatsApp or a student group chat, it looks the same as every other BorderPass link.

Do this: one designed share card per key page (title + who it's for + "Lawyer-reviewed"). Mock-ups are in section 05. Group chats are where international students share links, so this is a small fix with a big reach.
3

Page text arrives only after JavaScript runs

Each URL sends a correct title, description and canonical tag, then an empty <div id="root">. Google renders JavaScript, but many AI crawlers read only the raw HTML. To them, the PGWP page is a title and one sentence.

Do this: ask the product team about prerendering the top 40 public pages (static HTML snapshot at build time). For AI answers ("best way to apply for PGWP with a lawyer review"), being readable is step one. Plan in section 05.
4

No way to stay in touch before someone is ready to apply

The paths on the site lead to an assessment, an application or a demo. I couldn't find a newsletter sign-up or a free guide for a student who is 8 months away from applying.

Do this: a monthly "BorderPass Brief" plus a free "Am I ready?" checklist per pathway. Issue #1 is fully written in section 02.
5

One case study, many partnership stories

There is one employer case study (Global Construction Group). The news section mentions partnerships with Carleton University and Trent University, a #7 rank on The Globe and Mail's 2026 list of Canada's Top Growing Companies, and CCI's innovator network.

Do this: a case study series for each audience: one school, one employer, one agent, one student story (with consent). Template and interview questions in section 06.
6

CBIE 2026 is coming up

BorderPass is hosting an evening reception with Flywire and StudentVIP during CBIE 2026 in Ottawa on November 23. That room is full of international offices, the exact B2B audience.

Do this: a before, during and after content plan so one evening turns into a month of partner content and warm leads. It's built into the calendar.
7

Two handles across channels

Instagram and Facebook are borderpassclub. TikTok and YouTube are @borderpass. LinkedIn is borderpassai and X is @BorderPassAI.

Do this: same display name, photo and bio line everywhere, with each bio pointing to the others. People searching "BorderPass" on Instagram should land on the right account first try.
8

New destinations: US, UK and Australia

The navigation now lists Canada, United States, United Kingdom and Australia. The blog and social content are still almost all about Canada.

Do this: one pillar post and one social series per new destination, starting with "Canada vs UK vs Australia for your master's" style comparisons, which students search before they choose a country.

Brand and compliance guardrails for every piece

Always

  • Say "lawyer-reviewed", the core promise.
  • Date every policy post and link the official source.
  • Give the next step on every post.
  • Plain words; explain acronyms once (PGWP, GCMS, LMIA).

Never

  • Promise approval or quote odds for one person.
  • Give case-specific advice in comments or DMs.
  • Use fear to sell ("Don't get deported!").
  • Imply BorderPass is a law firm. It isn't; lawyers review the applications.

Voice

  • Calm older sibling who already did this.
  • Clear, warm, specific.
  • Confident about the system, careful about the law.

Proofreading
The 10-point pass
  1. Facts match the official source, dated.
  2. Program names spelled the official way.
  3. Numbers and fees double-checked.
  4. One idea per paragraph.
  5. Reading level around grade 8.
  6. Acronyms spelled out once.
  7. Every link works and opens the right page.
  8. Call to action matches the page.
  9. Alt text on every image.
  10. Read aloud once. If it sounds stiff, rewrite.

    
Content refresh
Keep 211 posts current
  • Monthly: pull posts losing clicks in Search Console.
  • Policy posts get a "what changed" box on top, not a new URL.
  • Merge posts that compete for the same search (two "study in Canada" guides exist).
  • Older posts like "Is PTE accepted in Canada in 2024" get updated titles and facts.
  • Every refreshed post links to its service page.
