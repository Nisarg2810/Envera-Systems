“Build around what already works.”

When I think about what the Social and Content Coordinator role could look like, I don't picture “running social media.” I picture telling the story of a company that started with one family restaurant in Barrie and is now three brands wide and growing by the week.

MHRA didn't get here by accident. Stacked scaled from one franchise to a hundred stores. Holy Chuck was brought in because it already had a following worth protecting. YLK & Batter is the newest chapter, and it's still being written. One philosophy runs through all three: build around what already works.

That's what I'd bring to social: create better content, understand what performs, learn from the audience, and do more of what works.

My BBA and my postgraduate certificate in Digital and Content Marketing give me the creative, customer-facing half. My postgraduate certificate in Information Systems Business Analysis gives me the other half: reading the numbers, spotting the pattern, and turning what we learn into the next decision.

I've worked across content, social, outreach, sales and customer acquisition, and I've sat on the side of marketing that asks a harder question than “does this look good?” The real question is “did this do anything for the business?”

A three-stage roadmap to build, test and grow

The first
90 days.

Built on insight. Fuelled by creative. Measured from week one.

01
Days 1–30

Listen, learn, audit.

  • A full social audit across all three brands: top and bottom performers, formats, posting patterns.
  • Comments and DMs read as research, plus a lightweight competitor watch.
  • A weekly KPI scoreboard goes live right away: reach, engagement, saves, follower growth, watch time.
02
Days 31–60

Build, test, create.

  • Each brand gets its own content system: pillars, tone and recurring formats built from the audit, not assumptions.
  • Test short-form hooks and video lengths.
  • Build an influencer list on genuine audience fit, and run shoots that produce several pieces each.
03
Days 61–90

Optimize, scale, grow.

  • Winning formats become series.
  • Paid support goes behind organic posts with proven signals, not guesswork.
  • A monthly review keeps the team honest: what worked, why, what didn't, what's next.

Months 4 to 12 · scale and build

The content
engine.

With real data in hand, the focus becomes more of what works, and smarter tests around what could. Ideas move from insight to shoot to performance to the next idea, building on what's been learned instead of starting from zero.

A new-store playbook

With a new store opening almost every week, content needs a repeatable launch kit: countdown content, opening-day coverage and local outreach, so growth never outruns the content that supports it. If Holy Chuck franchising lands, social becomes a recruitment tool too.

Social as customer insight

What people ask for and what keeps surfacing in the comments feeds menu decisions, promotions and future marketing, not just posts. And the portfolio becomes an advantage: a Stacked regular discovering Holy Chuck, a YLK & Batter fan hearing about the next Barrie opening.

By month 6

Three brands, three personalities, one content engine.

By year one

A recognizable identity across all three brands, a real creator network, and a content library built from a year of shoots.
