01 · GTM tracker

Every task has one owner and one date

Each task has an owner, a due date and whatever it’s waiting on. Click a status to move it along, or filter by team.

From the job post: “Coordinate go-to-market activities for product launches and campaigns, including timelines, task tracking, deliverables, and follow-ups.”
IDTaskTeamOwnerDueWaits onStatus

Rule I’d keep: if a task is blocked for 48 hours, it goes in the Friday report with a name next to it.

My thinking
  • Launches rarely slip on the big tasks. They slip on the small one nobody owned.
  • So every row has a person, not a team. You can’t chase “Channel”. You can chase a name.
  • “Waits on” is the column I’d watch most. It shows which task is holding up the next one, before it’s late.

03 · Launch checklist

The list for the things that get forgotten

Four phases. The French and Quebec items sit inside the list rather than at the end, because that’s where they usually get dropped.

“Launch checklists … help ensure campaign assets and messaging are delivered on schedule.”
My thinking
  • In Canada, French can’t be the last step. When it’s at the end, it’s the first thing cut when time runs short.
  • So each French item sits right next to its English one, in the same phase, with the same due date.
  • The list is split by phase, so on any day you only look at what matters that week.

08 · Budget tracker

Plan, committed and actual, side by side

Type into any “Actual” cell. A line more than 10% over turns red, and more than 25% under turns amber. The note to the Team Lead updates with it.

“Maintain the marketing budget tracker, monitoring spend against plan and flagging variances to the Product Marketing Team Lead.”
Line SampleTeamPlanCommittedActualVariance
Total
My thinking
  • Plan vs. actual alone shows a problem too late. Once a PO is raised, that money is gone, even before the invoice comes.
  • So I track committed as well. The gap between committed and actual is where surprises hide.
  • When one line runs over, I first look for one running under. The best fix keeps the total the same, so the Team Lead only has to say yes.
09 · POs, invoices, expenses

No invoice gets paid until it matches

A simple three-way check: the PO, the invoice and proof the work was delivered. If any of the three is missing or the amounts differ, the row stops.

“Assist with purchase requests, purchase orders, invoice processing, and expense reconciliation.”
POVendorForPO amountInvoiceInvoicedReceivedCheck

Purchase request

What, why, which budget line, amount with tax and the vendor quote attached. It goes to the budget owner before a PO is raised.

Month-end reconcile

Card expenses and invoices matched to budget lines by the 3rd working day. Anything without a receipt gets one reminder, then goes to the Team Lead.

Open commitments

POs raised but not yet invoiced are counted as spent in the tracker, so the budget never looks better than it really is.

My thinking
  • Paying an invoice that doesn’t match is easy to do and hard to undo.
  • So money moves only when three things agree: what we ordered, what they billed, and what arrived.
  • Open POs count as spent in the budget, so it never looks better than it is.
10 · Proof of execution

A co-op claim that gets paid the first time

A POE pack is easy to build while the campaign runs and hard to rebuild afterwards. The folder opens on launch day and fills up as things happen.

“Coordinate proof-of-execution (POE) reports for campaigns, events, and partner programs … and supporting claim and reimbursement submissions.”

The POE report, one page per activity

What goes to the partner or the regional team
  1. Activity: what ran, where, and on which dates
  2. Proof: dated screenshots and photos, one per placement
  3. Money: agreed amount, invoice and PO number
  4. Results: sell-through in the window against the 4 weeks before
  5. Sign-off: the channel lead’s name and date
Filing rule

POE / 2026-Q4 / CanadaComputers / ZB14-W2-Flyer / 01_artwork.pdf

Deadline I’d track

Q4 claims cut-off, Dec 31. The calendar sends a reminder 10 days before.

My thinking
  • Co-op claims get rejected for missing proof, not missing work.
  • Saving a dated screenshot takes ten seconds on the day. Finding it in January takes hours.
  • One file name pattern means anyone on the team can find the pack, not only me.

File name builder

Three rules

  • Only _CURRENT is shared. When a file is replaced, the old one moves to _ARCHIVE the same day.
  • Every English file has a French twin or a note saying why not.
  • On the first Monday of the month I check that links still work and the prices are still right.
My thinking
  • The most dangerous file is an old one that looks current.
  • One _CURRENT folder means a partner can never grab last month’s price.
  • Old files move to _ARCHIVE, never the bin. If someone asks what changed, we can show them.
