01 · The short version

The raw material is strong. The system around it can do more.

Limina already has hooks most B2B brands would pay for. The four changes below are about lining the ads up with each other and with where each reader is in the buying journey.

3

Great hooks are already here

"Is Hope a feeling or a patient?", "100% Compliant. 0% Useful." and "You cleared every structured field. The notes still name the patient." read like a clinician's real problem, not a vendor pitch.

ChangeTurn them from one-off ads into series. A strong hook is worth five creatives, not one.
2

Two accuracy numbers in one ad

Ad 1535868536 says 99.5% in the text and 99.96% in the image. Both may be true, since they measure different things, but the ad never says what each one measures.

ChangeLabel every number with what it measures, every time. A privacy buyer forwards the ad to legal, and legal will ask "99.5% of what?"

Seven ads end in "Talk to an expert" (or a technical, privacy or security expert). Only 3 point to the ungated sandbox, which is the lowest-friction, highest-proof offer Limina has.

ChangeCold readers get the sandbox. Warm readers get the call. Most readers on any given day are cold (rule 2 below).

ChangeBoost Patricia's posts. She is already a known voice in privacy, and people stop for faces, not logos.
02 · How the best do it

8 rules I checked every ad against

These rules come from LinkedIn's own ad specs, B2B marketing research and what the best B2B advertisers do. Each one has the technical reason, so it is not just taste.

1

Win the first 150 characters

On mobile, LinkedIn cuts the intro text and shows "...see more". Most people never tap it, so the hook and the reason to care must both sit before the cut.

Technical: LinkedIn's Sponsored Content specs recommend keeping intro text under 150 characters to avoid truncation, and headlines under about 70. In Limina's ad 1535868536, the visible part is the category promise plus "Talk to a Limina engineer (30 min, no deck):". The 99.5% proof point sits after the cut, where most readers never see it.
2

Speak to the 95, convert the 5

On any given day only about 5% of buyers are in market. The other 95% won't book a call, but they will remember you when the moment comes, if the ad tied Limina to that moment.

Technical: This is the 95:5 rule from Professor John Dawes (Ehrenberg-Bass), shared widely by the LinkedIn B2B Institute. For ads, it means cold audiences need memorable problem hooks and low-friction offers. "Book a call" asks the 95% for something they can't give yet.
3

Own the buying moments

People don't search for "de-identification". They hit a moment: "we're putting notes into an LLM", "security flagged a new vendor", "the redaction backlog is three weeks". Ads should name those moments word for word.

Technical: These moments are Category Entry Points (CEPs), a term coined by Jenni Romaniuk, who wrote the B2B Institute report on them. The brand that is linked to the most CEPs gets recalled first. Limina's best hooks (EHR notes, RAG pipeline) are already CEPs. Section 06 maps the rest.
4

Match the ask to the temperature

Cold readers get value with no strings (sandbox, guide). Warm readers (site visitors, video viewers, sandbox users) get the trial or the engineer call. Hot accounts get a message from a person.

Technical: LinkedIn Matched Audiences let you build each layer: website retargeting (Insight Tag), video viewers at 50%+, document ad openers, lead form openers and company lists for ABM. Each layer gets its own campaign with its own CTA, so the cost per result is measured against the right goal.
5

Proof = a number + a name + a method

"99.5%" is a claim. "99.5% accuracy on Providence Health's clinical notes" is proof. "Global pharma customer" reads as anonymous, and anonymous reads as unverified.

Technical: Privacy and security buyers are paid to doubt. Every number they can't trace becomes a question in the security review, which slows the deal down. A traceable number (customer, dataset, method or validator) answers the question before it is asked.
6

One idea per ad

A scroller gives an ad about a second. One idea gets read. Six ideas get skipped together.

Technical: The 1535868536 creative holds six claims: headline, accuracy, on-prem, four formats, speed and the call offer. The "100% Compliant" creative holds one. It is no surprise which one stops the thumb.
7

Same look, every time

Limina's ads already look like a family: navy, electric blue, logo top-left. That's a real asset. Add one recurring device that only Limina uses: the redaction bar, which becomes a tag.

Technical: Distinctive brand assets (colour, shape, type, a repeated device) let people recognise the brand without reading the logo. The B2B Institute's brand research calls this out as how ad memory builds across exposures.
8

Judge ads on pipeline, not clicks

High CTR feels good, but it isn't revenue. Track sandbox runs, account reach inside the target list, meetings and pipeline.

Technical: In ZenABM's 2026 dataset (211 B2B companies), CTR had a slightly negative link to pipeline (Spearman rho = -0.17), while spend had a positive one (rho = 0.57). Compare formats on eCTR (landing clicks only), because thought leader ad CTR also counts likes and "see more" taps.

Sources: ZenABM, LinkedIn Ads Benchmarks 2026 (vendor data, 161,256 ads) · ZenABM, Thought Leader Ads guide · Dreamdata on the 95:5 rule (John Dawes) · LinkedIn B2B Institute, Category Entry Points in a B2B World · ClickMinded, LinkedIn Ads Benchmarks

Why name the customerA named global pharma brand does two jobs. It proves the claim, and it borrows trust ("if their compliance team approved Limina, mine can too"). Anonymous proof does neither.

TEST A

Call vs sandbox, same creative

Run the Hope ad with "Talk to an expert" vs "Try this sentence". Judge on meetings and pipeline at 60 days, not clicks. This one test settles whether sandbox-first works for Limina.

TEST B

Anonymous vs named proof

"Global pharma customer" vs "Boehringer Ingelheim" on the same layout. Measures how much a name is worth with this audience.

TEST C

Company page vs Patricia

The same message posted from the Limina page and boosted from Patricia. Compare eCTR and sandbox runs, not raw CTR.

TEST D

Number with method vs bare number

"99.5%" vs "99.5% on real clinical notes" vs "99.96%, validated by Aetion". Finds the proof format a privacy buyer trusts most.

House rules for every test

One variable per test. Run each arm for at least 2 weeks and 100+ clicks before calling it (my working rule, so one good day doesn't decide anything). Refresh a creative when its CTR falls about 20% below its first two weeks, or when frequency in the same audience climbs past about 4 in a month. Today the free trial ad shows up 3 times in the 22 newest results, so it's a refresh candidate.

08 · Before any ad ships

A 10-point checklist

  • The hook and the reason to care fit in the first 150 characters.
  • The headline is under 70 characters and doesn't just repeat the image.
  • One idea per creative. Count the claims, and keep it to one or two.
  • Every number says what it measures (dataset, customer or validator).
  • The same number appears the same way everywhere (99.5% vs 99.96%).
  • The CTA matches the layer: cold gets the sandbox or guide, hot gets the call.
  • The button, headline and image all ask for the same thing.
  • Product output in a creative is real sandbox output, not a mock.
  • No em dashes or emoji bullets in the first lines. They read as templated.
  • UTM tags are on, so the tracker can tie the ad to sandbox runs and pipeline.
