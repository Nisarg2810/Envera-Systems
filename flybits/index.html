01

Much of your content is invisible to crawlers that don't run JavaScript.

High
What I found

Your pages ship as templates. The raw HTML contains nearly 1,600 placeholders like {{ a.title }} and {{ st.num }} across the 16 sitemap pages, and JavaScript fills them in after the page loads. People see the finished page. But many crawlers read only the raw HTML, and most AI crawlers (the ones behind ChatGPT, Claude and Perplexity) don't run JavaScript. They see the placeholders.

Share of words a no-JavaScript crawler can see

Homepage682 / 991
Customer stories399 / 1,047
News & thinking115 / 548

So on the page that holds your customer stories, 62% of the words never reach those crawlers. On the News page, it's 79%. The story you most want AI assistants to repeat is the hardest one for them to read.

Check it yourself 
# raw HTML: what a no-JS crawler gets
curl -s https://flybits.com/news-and-thinking | grep -c '{{'

# rendered: what a browser shows
chrome --headless --dump-dom https://flybits.com/news-and-thinking
The fix
  • Render at build time. The site is already static .html files, so the data can be written into the HTML when you build, instead of in the browser. Keep the JavaScript for interaction only.
  • If that's a bigger change, prerender just the 16 sitemap pages and the articles first.
  • Then re-test every page with the same two commands and keep the check in your deploy routine.
Who: developerSize: one build-step changeMe: test plan + page-by-page QA
What it changes

Every word on every page becomes readable to every crawler. That's the precondition for being quoted in AI answers and for Google indexing your content on the first pass. It doesn't guarantee citations. It removes the thing that blocks them.

05

There are two copies of the site.

Medium
What I found

https://flybits.com/ and https://www.flybits.com/ both return a full page (HTTP 200). Neither redirects to the other, and there's no canonical tag to say which one counts. Plain http://www.flybits.com goes to the www version, while the sitemap lists the non-www version.

Check it yourself 
curl -sI https://flybits.com/      | head -1   # 200
curl -sI https://www.flybits.com/  | head -1   # 200
The fix

Pick one (the sitemap already uses flybits.com). 301 every www URL to it, and add self-referencing canonicals (finding 02).

Who: devSize: one rule
What it changes

Links and signals pile up on one version instead of being split across two. Analytics stop splitting too.

Agentic commerce in Canada: FAQ

For the owned explainer page, with FAQPage schema. Facts are from the Mastercard release and its coverage.

What is agentic commerce?

Agentic commerce is shopping where an AI agent acts for a person within limits they set. The agent can find a product, check it against a budget and complete the payment, with the customer in control of what it's allowed to do.

What was Canada's first agentic commerce transaction?

In September 2026, a Flybits-powered AI assistant acted on instructions from a Rogers Red Mastercard cardholder. It found an eligible product within a set budget and completed the purchase using Mastercard Agent Pay.

Who did what in the transaction?

Flybits provided the AI assistant and the reasoning behind its recommendation. Rogers Bank provided the card account. Mastercard processed the payment on its network through Mastercard Agent Pay.

Can Rogers Bank customers use it today?

Not yet. Rogers has said the service is in testing and isn't available to customers yet.

Why does agentic commerce matter to card issuers?

If agents start making purchases, the card the agent is set up to use wins the transaction. Issuers that can support agent payments, with clear customer controls, can stay at the centre of how their cardholders shop. (Draft framing, to review with your team.)

How does Flybits keep the customer in control?

Needs your team's input. I'd answer this with the specific controls (budgets, approvals, explanations of recommendations) in your own words, because it's the question risk teams will ask first.
