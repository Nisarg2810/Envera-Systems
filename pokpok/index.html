The pay calendar

Two pay runs a month, so nobody waits more than two weeks.

  • 1st and 15th: cut-off. Approved content and affiliate sales up to here are in.
  • 3rd and 18th: I reconcile the roster against approvals and affiliate reports, and flag anything odd.
  • 4th and 19th: my manager signs off on one summary line per creator.
  • 5th and 20th: paid, plus a short "you've been paid" note with the breakdown.

Before anyone is paid

  • Content is approved and live (or delivered, for ads-only)
  • The amount matches the contract or the affiliate report
  • Payment details and tax form are on file
  • Usage rights end date is in the roster
  • Anything over budget goes to my manager first

Accurate beats fast, but on time is the promise. If a payment slips, the creator hears it from me before they have to ask.

07 · Discover talent, test fresh angles

Six angles to test, and how I'd scout

From the post: "Discover new creators and test fresh creative angles to keep our content feeling authentic and inspiring."

The 20 minutes I need

"How I get dinner made."

Split screen: parent cooking real dinner, child cooking in the Cooking toy. Honest, relatable, and very calm.

"What did you make?"

Let the kid do the talking.

Ask one of our learning prompts on camera and let the child explain. No script can beat a 4-year-old's explanation.

Off the screen too

"Then she built a real one."

After Marble Machine, the child builds a marble run from paper rolls. Proof the play keeps going after the iPad is down.

The switch-off test

"Watch what happens when I say time's up."

The calm transition parents hope for. Only real moments, never staged, and only creators whose kids really do it.

Same toy, two ages

"My 2-year-old vs my 6-year-old."

Siblings play the same toy in completely different ways. Shows "grows with kids" without saying it.

The educator explains

"An OT on what this toy is building."

A teacher, OT or ECE breaks down one toy in 30 seconds. Trusted voices, and a great fit with your educator team.

SOP: paying a creator

  1. On the 1st or 15th, filter the roster for "approved, not paid".
  2. Match each one to the contract amount, or the affiliate report for sales.
  3. Check payment details, tax form and usage dates are filled in.
  4. Send my manager one summary: name, amount, reason. Anything unusual on top.
  5. Pay on the 5th or 20th and mark "paid" with the date.
  6. Email each creator their breakdown the same day.

What I'd flag as volume grows

Applications by form, not email. The new affiliate page sorts people before anyone opens an inbox.

Three contract templates. UGC, ads-only and affiliate, so every deal starts the same way.

One source of truth. One roster that the pay run, the reports and the ads team all read from.

A creator newsletter. One monthly "what's working" email instead of the same tips in fifty DMs.

Brand partnerships, beyond Yoto

Indigo. A physical Pok Pok gift card on the shelf in the kids' section for the holidays. Your "Redeem your code" page is already built for it.
Toronto Public Library. Free family playtest mornings in branches, the same way you already playtest with kids in your community.
Montessori schools. A "school family" code for parents, with an educator night hosted by one of your educators.
